================================================================================
MICROBIOME-WIDE ASSOCIATION STUDY (MWAS) - SUMMARY
================================================================================

OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Identify species-level associations between urban microbiome composition
and economic development (USA vs Upper-middle-income) while accounting
for geographic confounders.

METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Approach: Linear regression (two models per species)
  Model 1 (Unadjusted): CLR ~ Group
  Model 2 (Adjusted):   CLR ~ Group + Latitude + Elevation

Sample size: 135 samples with complete covariate data
  USA (Control): 29
  Upper-middle (Case): 106

Species tested: 134
Multiple testing correction: FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg)
Significance threshold: FDR q < 0.05

================================================================================
KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. ROBUST ECONOMIC ASSOCIATIONS:
   Species: 0 (0.0%)
   Interpretation: TRUE economic effects, independent of geography

2. GEOGRAPHIC CONFOUNDING:
   Species: 86 (64.2%)
   Interpretation: Associations were GEOGRAPHIC, not economic

3. SUPPRESSOR EFFECTS:
   Species: 0 (0.0%)
   Interpretation: Economic effect was masked by geography

4. NO ASSOCIATION:
   Species: 48 (35.8%)
   Interpretation: No significant differences

================================================================================
BIOLOGICAL INTERPRETATION
================================================================================

No significant associations found after FDR correction.
Geographic factors dominate over economic effects.

CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

LIMITED ECONOMIC SIGNAL DETECTED:

- Only 0.0% of species show robust economic associations
- Geographic factors appear to dominate over economic effects
- USA vs East Asia comparison is primarily geographic
- Economic development effects are modest

SEVERE GEOGRAPHIC CONFOUNDING:

- 64.2% of associations were confounded
- Unadjusted analyses would be highly misleading
- Covariate adjustment was essential for valid inference
- Results highlight importance of controlling for geography
